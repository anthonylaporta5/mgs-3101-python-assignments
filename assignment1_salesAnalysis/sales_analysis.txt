Coffee Shop Sales Analysis

The coffee shop sales dataset contains transaction-level information for Maven Roasters, a fictional coffee shop business operating at three locations in New York City. The dataset covers January through June 2023 and contains 149,116 transactions. The data includes the transaction date and time, quantity purchased, store location, product category, product type, product detail, and unit price. This information makes it possible to examine when customers purchase products, which products generate the most revenue, and how sales differ between locations.

One important finding is that sales are strongest during the morning hours. Analysis of the dataset shows that 10:00 AM is the peak hour by transaction volume. This suggests that the business experiences a significant morning rush and should make sure that enough employees are scheduled during this period. A data-backed recommendation would be to increase staffing and make sure popular products are fully stocked before the morning rush. This could reduce customer wait times and prevent lost sales caused by products being unavailable.

A second recommendation is to focus marketing and inventory decisions on the products and categories that generate the most revenue. Coffee is the largest revenue-producing product category in the dataset, generating approximately $270,000 in sales during the six-month period. Barista Espresso is also one of the strongest individual products by revenue. Rather than treating every product equally, the company could give more attention to its strongest products through promotions, better product placement, and maintaining adequate inventory. At the same time, products with lower sales could be reviewed to determine whether they should be improved, promoted differently, or eventually removed.

A third recommendation is to compare performance between the three store locations. The dataset includes Astoria, Hell's Kitchen, and Lower Manhattan, allowing management to identify differences in revenue and customer demand. One analysis of the dataset found that Hell's Kitchen generated the highest revenue among the three locations. Management could use this information to investigate what is causing differences between stores, including customer traffic, product preferences, operating hours, and local demand. Successful practices from higher-performing locations could potentially be tested at the other stores.

The data also shows overall growth during the six-month period. Total revenue across the dataset is approximately $698,812, and revenue increased substantially from the beginning of the period toward June. This suggests that the business was experiencing increasing sales during the period being analyzed. However, six months of data is still a relatively short period, so additional years of information would be useful before assuming that the growth will continue at the same rate.

There are several questions I would have after completing this analysis. First, I would want to know why sales are highest at certain times of day and whether the pattern is consistent throughout the week. I would also want to know whether the same products are popular at every location or whether customers in different neighborhoods have different preferences. Another question would be whether promotions or price changes affected sales during the six-month period. Finally, I would want to know the profit margin for each product rather than only its revenue, because a product that generates high revenue is not necessarily the most profitable product.

Overall, the data provides useful information about customer purchasing patterns, product performance, timing, and location performance. The main recommendations are to schedule employees and prepare inventory around peak morning demand, focus marketing and inventory on high-performing products and categories, and compare the three locations to identify practices that could improve performance. These recommendations use measurable sales information rather than relying only on assumptions about customer behavior.
